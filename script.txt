MANDATE — 3-MINUTE DEMO SCRIPT
================================
Total runtime budget: 3:00
  Part 1 — Product pitch : ~1:00
  Part 2 — Live demo     : ~2:00

------------------------------------------------
PART 1 — PRODUCT PITCH (~1:00, read this)
------------------------------------------------
AI agents are starting to hold real permissions and real assets on
chain. But if the wallet controlling one gets compromised, or the
agent itself misbehaves, there's no second check. Whoever holds the
key can change anything about it, instantly, with one signature.

Mandate fixes that. Every agent gets a real identity: ERC-8004 plus a
human-readable ENS name. From there, any change to its ownership or
its permissions goes through a PermissionGate. It sits blocked as
Pending until a separate approver signs it, on a physically connected
Ledger. Nothing executes until that second signature happens, and it
can be rejected outright instead.

And it's fully self-serve. Connect your own wallet, register your own
agent, deploy your own gate naming your own approver. No backend
holding your keys, no shared demo setup. Every agent also carries
public reputation feedback, so trust isn't just assumed, it's visible.

This is live on Sepolia right now: real identity registry, real
contracts, real subgraph indexing every action.

------------------------------------------------
PART 2 — LIVE DEMO FLOW (~2:00, read-only — no live writes)
------------------------------------------------
Nothing below submits a transaction. Every screen is either a live
read off Sepolia or an already-settled past action. Keeps the demo
tight and removes all confirmation-wait risk.

Before you start recording:
  - Have an agent with real history already loaded (agent1.mandate.eth
    has a Pending action + several Executed ones).
  - Have a wallet already connected (just for the UI state, you won't
    sign anything) so "Connect wallet" doesn't eat time on camera.
  - Keep a Sepolia Etherscan tab pre-opened in the background.

What to showcase, in order:

1. Landing page — one glance, one line, click "Launch app."
   SAY: "Mandate gives every on-chain agent a real identity, ERC-8004
   plus a human-readable ENS name, then puts any change to its
   ownership behind a second, hardware-signed approval."

2. Agents dashboard — the grid of real on-chain agents: identity,
   live status (Confirmed/Pending/Blocked), reputation score. Use the
   search box and a status filter pill to show it's real filtering.
   SAY: "Every card is a real registered identity, not mock data —
   including reputation feedback other users have actually left
   on-chain for it."

3. Agent detail — current owner, capability manifest, gated-action
   history table. Click through to Etherscan.
   SAY: "The owner here is a live read straight off the chain, not a
   cached value. The capability manifest comes from an ENS text
   record. And this whole action history is indexed by a subgraph,
   not our own database — every row traces back to a real event."

4. Register — open /register and walk through the three self-serve
   steps without submitting: connect wallet, register identity,
   deploy your own gate.
   SAY: "Registration is fully self-serve. Connect your own wallet,
   mint your agent's ERC-8004 identity with its ENS name, then deploy
   your own PermissionGate naming your own hardware approver — every
   one of these is a direct call from your wallet, nothing runs
   through a backend key. I'll skip actually submitting to keep this
   tight."

5. Propose a change — open the Propose form, show the fields, don't
   submit. Then navigate straight to the agent's existing Pending
   action instead.
   SAY: "A proposed change, an ownership transfer or a permission
   escalation, routes through the PermissionGate and sits blocked as
   Pending. Here's one doing exactly that right now."

6. The payoff — an agent with an already-Executed action in its
   history. Click into it, then over to the Etherscan tab.
   SAY: "Nothing executes without a second signature: a physically
   signed, typed-data approval on hardware. This one's already been
   signed and executed — real transaction, right here on Etherscan."

Buffer: if you're running long, cut step 4 (Register) down to a
single sentence over the dashboard instead of opening the page — it's
the most skippable beat.
